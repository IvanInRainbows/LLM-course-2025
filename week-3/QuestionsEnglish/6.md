7. My boss is trying to take credit for my work. What to do? :
It’s frustrating when someone takes credit for your hard work, but there are professional and strategic ways to handle this situation. Here’s a step-by-step approach:

### **1. Stay Calm & Assess the Situation**
- **Don’t react emotionally**—take a moment to process your feelings before responding.
- **Gather evidence**: Save emails, messages, project files, or any documentation that proves your contributions.
- **Determine intent**: Is this a one-time oversight, or a pattern? Is your boss intentionally stealing credit, or just unaware of your role?

### **2. Subtly Reclaim Credit (If Appropriate)**
If the situation is minor or unintentional, you can **politely correct the record** without confrontation:
- **In meetings**:
  - *"I’m glad we could contribute to this project—I especially enjoyed working on [specific part] because [reason]."*
  - *"As I mentioned earlier, my approach to [task] was [explanation], which helped achieve [result]."*
- **In emails**:
  - CC relevant stakeholders when sharing updates: *"Following up on my work on [X], here’s the latest progress…"*
  - Use phrases like: *"Based on my research/analysis, we decided to…"*

### **3. Have a Private Conversation (If Needed)**
If this is a recurring issue, **schedule a 1:1 with your boss** (keep it professional and non-accusatory):
- **Frame it as collaboration**:
  > *"I noticed in the last meeting that my contributions to [project] weren’t highlighted. I’d love to make sure my work aligns with your expectations—could we discuss how to better communicate my role in future updates?"*
- **Ask for feedback**:
  > *"I want to ensure I’m contributing effectively. Could you help me understand how I can take more ownership in presenting my work?"*
- **Set expectations**:
  > *"Moving forward, I’d appreciate it if my contributions to [X] were acknowledged, as it helps with my professional growth."*

### **4. Escalate Strategically (If Necessary)**
If your boss **consistently** takes credit and it’s affecting your career:
- **Document everything** (emails, meeting notes, project logs).
- **Build alliances**: Ensure key stakeholders (peers, other managers) know your contributions.
- **Go to HR (last resort)**:
  - If this is part of a larger pattern of unethical behavior, HR may intervene—but only if you have **clear evidence** and are prepared for potential backlash.

### **5. Protect Your Future Work**
- **Proactively communicate**: Send recap emails after meetings summarizing your contributions.
- **Take ownership in visible ways**: Volunteer to present your work in meetings or write project summaries.
- **Build a reputation outside your boss’s sphere**: Network with other leaders who can vouch for your skills.

### **6. Decide If It’s Worth Staying**
If this is part of a **toxic work culture** where credit-theft is normalized:
- **Start looking for another job**—a boss who steals credit is unlikely to advocate for your growth.
- **Use this as a learning experience**: In future roles, set clearer expectations early on.

### **Example Scripts**
**For a meeting where your boss takes credit:**
> *"Actually, I led the research on that part—happy to share more details if helpful!"*

**For a 1:1 with your boss:**
> *"I’ve really enjoyed working on [project], and I’d love to make sure my contributions are clear to the team. How can I better highlight my role in these discussions?"*

### **Key Takeaways**
✅ **Act professionally**—avoid public confrontation.
✅ **Document everything**—protect yourself with evidence.
✅ **Build visibility**—ensure others know your work.
✅ **Know when to leave**—if this is a pattern, it may not be worth fighting.
