8. Traditionally, what do you eat for Easter? :
Easter is celebrated with a variety of traditional foods around the world, often tied to religious symbolism, springtime harvests, or regional customs. Here are some of the most common Easter dishes and treats:

### **1. Lamb (Symbol of Christ)**
   - **Roast Lamb** – A centerpiece in many cultures (e.g., Greece, Italy, France, the Middle East, and the U.S.), symbolizing Jesus as the "Lamb of God."
   - **Greek Magiritsa** – A lamb offal soup eaten after midnight Easter services.
   - **Italian Abbacchio** – Roasted or braised lamb, often served with artichokes.

### **2. Eggs (Symbol of Rebirth & Resurrection)**
   - **Decorated Eggs** – Hard-boiled eggs dyed in bright colors (common in Eastern Europe, like **Ukrainian Pysanky** or **Russian Krashenki**).
   - **Egg Hunts & Chocolate Eggs** – Popular in Western countries (U.S., UK, Australia), often filled with candy.
   - **Deviled Eggs** – A common dish in the Southern U.S.
   - **Egg-Based Dishes**:
     - **Spanish Torrijas** (like French toast, soaked in milk and honey).
     - **Italian Frittata** or **Torta Pasqualina** (a savory pie with eggs and greens).
     - **Polish Żurek** (sour rye soup with hard-boiled eggs).

### **3. Bread & Pastries (Symbol of Life & Prosperity)**
   - **Hot Cross Buns** (UK, Australia, Commonwealth) – Spiced buns with a cross, eaten on Good Friday.
   - **Easter Bread**:
     - **Italian Panettone di Pasqua** or **Colomba di Pasqua** (dove-shaped cake).
     - **Greek Tsoureki** (sweet braided bread with red-dyed eggs).
     - **Russian Kulich** (tall, cylindrical Easter bread) with **Paskha** (a sweet cheese dessert).
     - **Polish Babka** (a tall, sweet yeast cake).
   - **Simnel Cake** (UK) – A fruit cake with marzipan, decorated with 11 marzipan balls (representing the apostles minus Judas).

### **4. Ham (Symbol of Abundance)**
   - **Glazed Ham** – Popular in the U.S., Germany, and Scandinavia, often served with mustard or honey glaze.
   - **German Osterschinken** – A special Easter ham.

### **5. Cheese & Dairy (End of Lent Fasting)**
   - **Paskha (Russia)** – A pyramid-shaped cheese dessert made with farmer’s cheese, butter, and raisins.
   - **French Fromage de Pâques** – Special Easter cheeses.
   - **Scandinavian Smörgåsbord** – Includes herring, smoked salmon, and cheeses.

### **6. Sweet Treats**
   - **Chocolate Bunnies & Eggs** – Ubiquitous in Western countries (especially Germany, Switzerland, and the U.S.).
   - **Italian Pastiera Napoletana** – A ricotta and wheat berry pie.
   - **Portuguese Folar de Páscoa** – A sweet bread with hard-boiled eggs baked inside.
   - **Mexican Capirotada** – A bread pudding with cinnamon and cheese, eaten during Lent and Easter.

### **7. Regional Specialties**
   - **Poland/Ukraine**: **Barszcz Biały** (white borscht) with sausage and eggs.
   - **Hungary**: **Sonkás-Krémes** (ham and custard pastry).
   - **Sweden**: **Äggröra** (egg butter spread) on crispbread.
   - **Ethiopia**: **Doro Wat** (spicy chicken stew) after fasting for Lent.
   - **Jamaica**: **Easter Bun** (spiced sweet bread) with cheese.

### **Why These Foods?**
- **Lamb & Eggs** = Sacrifice and rebirth (Christian symbolism).
- **Bread & Sweets** = Celebration after Lent’s fasting.
- **Ham** = Historically, pigs were slaughtered in autumn and cured by spring.
