13. I do not like that my partner keeps in touch with his ex. How can I make him stop?:
It’s completely normal to feel uncomfortable about your partner staying in touch with an ex, but it’s important to approach this situation with trust, communication, and respect—not control. Here’s how you can handle it in a healthy way:

### **1. Reflect on Your Feelings First**
Before addressing it with your partner, ask yourself:
- **Why does this bother me?** (Is it jealousy, insecurity, past trauma, or genuine red flags?)
- **Is their contact appropriate?** (Are they just civil, or is there emotional/romantic involvement?)
- **Do I trust my partner?** (If not, the issue may be deeper than just the ex.)

If your discomfort comes from insecurity, work on building self-confidence. If it’s because their behavior is disrespectful (e.g., secrecy, emotional affairs), that’s a different conversation.

### **2. Have an Open, Non-Accusatory Conversation**
Instead of demanding they cut off contact (which can backfire), try:
- **"I’ve been feeling a little uncomfortable about your contact with [ex’s name]. Can we talk about it?"**
- **"I trust you, but I’d love to understand why you stay in touch and how it makes you feel."**
- **"Are there boundaries we can set that would make us both comfortable?"**

Avoid ultimatums ("Stop talking to them or else!")—this can make your partner defensive and may push them closer to their ex.

### **3. Set Mutual Boundaries (If Needed)**
If their contact is excessive or makes you uneasy, discuss **healthy boundaries**, such as:
- **No late-night or secretive conversations**
- **No discussing relationship problems with the ex**
- **No meeting up one-on-one if it feels inappropriate**
- **Transparency (e.g., not hiding messages, but not demanding access to their phone)**

If your partner cares about your feelings, they’ll be willing to adjust—**as long as your requests are reasonable**.

### **4. Observe Their Response**
- **If they listen, reassure you, and adjust their behavior**, that’s a good sign.
- **If they dismiss your feelings, get defensive, or refuse to compromise**, ask yourself: *Is this someone who respects my emotional needs?*

### **5. Work on Trust & Security in the Relationship**
If the issue is more about your insecurity than their actions:
- **Build self-esteem** (therapy, hobbies, friendships outside the relationship).
- **Focus on your connection**—plan dates, deepen intimacy, and remind yourself why you trust them.
- **Avoid snooping**—it erodes trust and creates tension.

### **6. Know When to Walk Away**
If your partner:
✔ **Prioritizes their ex over your comfort** (e.g., refuses to set boundaries)
✔ **Lies or hides their interactions**
✔ **Still has romantic/emotional ties to their ex**
…then this may be a **dealbreaker**. You deserve someone who respects your feelings and chooses you wholeheartedly.

### **What NOT to Do:**
❌ **Demand they block the ex** (unless there’s cheating/inappropriate behavior).
❌ **Check their phone/social media** (this creates distrust).
❌ **Compare yourself to the ex** (it fuels insecurity).
❌ **Punish them** (silent treatment, threats—this pushes people away).

### **Final Thought:**
You can’t (and shouldn’t) *control* who your partner talks to, but you **can** control:
- How you communicate your needs
- Whether you stay with someone who doesn’t respect them
- How you build your own confidence and security

If this is a recurring issue causing major distress, couples therapy could help. But if your partner is truly loyal and their contact with the ex is harmless, learning to trust them may be the best path forward.
