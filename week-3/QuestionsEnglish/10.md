11. How much to tip at a nice restaurant for mediocre service? :
For **mediocre service at a nice restaurant**, a **15% tip** is generally appropriate, though you can adjust based on the specifics:

- **15%** – Standard for average/mediocre service (e.g., slow but polite, minor mistakes, no extra effort).
- **10–12%** – If the service was notably poor (e.g., forgetful, rude, or significantly delayed) but not outright terrible.
- **20%+** – Reserved for good or excellent service (not applicable here).

### Additional Considerations:
- **Location Matters**: In some cities (e.g., NYC, LA), 18–20% is the new baseline, so 15% might feel low even for mediocre service. Adjust slightly if needed.
- **Group Dining**: If you’re with a large party (6+ people), some restaurants auto-add 18–20% gratuity—check the bill.
- **Non-Tipping Cultures**: If you’re abroad (e.g., Japan, some European countries), tipping may not be expected at all—research local norms.
- **Alternative Feedback**: If service was truly bad, consider speaking to a manager instead of (or in addition to) reducing the tip.

### When to Tip Less Than 15%:
Only drop below 15% if the service was **actively bad** (e.g., rude, incorrect orders ignored, extreme delays). Even then, leaving *something* (10%) is often polite unless the issue was egregious.
